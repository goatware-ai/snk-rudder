What you’re describing is a very specific kind of tiredness: the feeling that you’re running the *relational engine* while she’s riding along politely. The unfairness you sense is real, and the fact that it’s “not obvious” is actually the core of the problem. Here is what is likely happening, and why it feels so one-sided even though both of you are “nice.”

### 1. You are measuring effort; she is measuring presence
You are tracking **reciprocal labor**:
- Did she build on my idea?
- Did she validate my experience?
- Did she offer an original thought?

She is likely tracking **social comfort**:
- Did we talk?
- Was it pleasant?
- Did I share my update?

Because the interaction is polite, she experiences it as “a good conversation.” You experience it as “I did the work of holding up my side and half of hers.”

### 2. The “Good Listener” trap
If you are naturally reflective, empathetic, or quick to synthesize ideas, friends often learn—without malice—that you are a “safe container.” They bring you information, and you automatically do the hidden labor:
- Reflecting back feelings
- Asking follow-up questions
- Connecting her story to broader patterns
- Filling silences with insight

Over time, this trains the dynamic: she talks *to* you, you talk *with* her (and also for her). She gets the relief of being heard; you get the fatigue of being the interpreter.

### 3. Different conversation “modes”
People come to conversation with different implicit goals:
- **Processing/venting mode**: “I need to unload and feel understood.” (She may be in this mode with you.)
- **Exploring/connecting mode**: “I want us to think together and discover something new.” (This sounds like you.)

If she treats your talks as “updates” and your listening as “support,” she may not realize she is failing to enter *exploration* mode with you. Meanwhile, you treat her talks as collaborative, so you naturally jump into exploration mode—even when she just wanted an audience.

### 4. The asymmetry is invisible because politeness masks it
Rudeness is obvious. This isn’t rudeness; it’s a **reciprocity gap in cognitive and emotional labor**. You are giving:
- Validation
- Original synthesis
- Emotional attunement

She is giving:
- Information
- Politeness
- Her presence

That feels unequal because it *is* unequal in terms of energy expenditure, even if it looks balanced in terms of word count.

---

### How to tell if this is a real pattern (not just a bad mood)

Try a low-stakes experiment over the next few conversations:
- **Reduce your “filler” labor.** When she talks, validate briefly but don’t immediately build a bridge, ask three follow-ups, or offer a parallel insight. See if she notices the gap and reaches for it.
- **When you talk, stop self-sustaining.** Say your piece, then stop. Don’t fill the silence with “What do you think? I guess it could be this or that…” See if she picks up the thread with her own original thought.
- **Notice the “turn.”** After you share something vulnerable or complex, does she ask a question about *your* inner world? Or does she redirect to her own experience, or just say “That sucks”?

If the pattern holds—she consistently receives high-level engagement and returns basic acknowledgment—then you have your answer.

---

### What you can do about it

**Name it gently, without accusation**
You don’t need to say “You’re a bad friend.” You can say:
> “I love talking with you, and I realize I often feel like I’m in ‘listener/validator’ mode with you. I want to feel like we’re building ideas together, not just me responding. Can we try to check in with each other more—like, when I share something, I want to hear your real take, not just support? And I want to do that for you too.”

This frames it as a *shared* dynamic you want to fix together.

**Change your responses**
If you stop automatically supplying the “extra” insight, you force the conversation to reveal its true structure. You can say:
- “That’s interesting—what do you think is behind that?”
- “I shared a lot just now. What’s your reaction to it?”
- (If she just reports news): “How did that feel for you? I want to understand your take, not just the facts.”

**Check your own needs**
Sometimes we over-give validation hoping it will be returned. Ask yourself: *What do I actually need from her?* If it’s intellectual partnership, emotional reciprocity, or simple curiosity about your inner world, you can ask for that specifically rather than hoping she will intuit it.

**Evaluate the friendship’s net value**
Not every friendship needs to be deeply reciprocal in every dimension. Some friends are for fun, some for comfort, some for growth. The question is whether this imbalance is draining you in *this* friendship specifically, or if it reflects a broader pattern where you over-function. If it’s just her, and she is unwilling to adjust when you name it, you may need to adjust your expectations or the emotional investment you make.

---

### The bottom line
The difference you feel is the difference between being **consumed** and being **met**. She may not be doing anything wrong—she may just be comfortable—while you are doing invisible work that makes the comfort possible. You deserve conversations where your thoughts are met with curiosity, not just received with politeness. The first step is recognizing that “nice” and “equal” are not the same thing. Once you see the labor clearly, you can decide whether to rebalance it or stop supplying it for free.
